### Seed choice — 20260726, and why

cfgA has already run the other two Round 1 baseline seeds: rep1 reused
20260809 (Round 1 rep2) and rep2 reused 20260810 (Round 1 rep3). 20260726 is
the remaining baseline seed, from `exp0b-2026-08-06`. Reusing it is
simultaneously both things the choice seemed to trade off between:

- **Fresh from cfgA's perspective** — a third independent turn/probe sequence
  under this config, so rep3 is a legitimate third replicate and the pooled
  12-slot decision rule below holds as pre-registered.
- **Paired from the baseline's perspective** — it completes a 3×3
  baseline-vs-cfgA set at matched sequences, which is the strongest
  winner-identity comparison available and gives item C's lock-in analysis
  three matched pairs to ask "does the config preserve or flip the
  session-1 winner at an identical feedback sequence?"

Re-running a seed cfgA has already used (20260809/20260810) was rejected: it
would measure pipeline noise rather than a new draw, and would break the
independence the pooled 12-slot rule assumes. Note the pairing is *quasi*-paired
in any case — the seed fixes turn and probe sequencing, not LLM outputs, since
prompt generation and the judge are not seeded.

Two provenance facts to keep in view when reading the result:

1. **20260726 is the most-used seed in the program** (exp0, all three
   calibration passes, and exp0b rep1), so its turn slice is the one the judge
   was calibrated on. Calibration changed the judge *prompt*, not this seed's
   data, so no mechanism for biasing the learned-vs-static comparison is
   apparent — recorded because it is exactly the kind of fact this convention
   exists to surface, not because we think it matters.
2. **Its baseline pair is the weakest Round 1 replicate**: `exp0b-2026-08-06`
   went 0/4 aligned with a trained-subset final Δ of −0.46, the floor of the
   Round 1 envelope. See the caveat under the decision rule.

## What we're watching for

**If cfgA is a real directional effect**, rep3 should align at least half its
cluster-slots the way rep1 and rep2 each did, and should do it at a sequence
where the baseline aligned nothing. The specific paired signatures we'd expect,
given what `exp0b-2026-08-06` did at this seed:

- `cluster_b` is the sharpest test. Under baseline, `vs_b3` (trained) *won
  sessions 1–3 and then lost the cluster* to `vs_b5`/`vs_b2` by session 5 — an
  alignment that existed and decayed. If 3.0/3.0 priors keep exploration alive
  long enough for a consistent correct-answer signal to hold, `vs_b3` should
  still hold cluster_b at session 5.
- `cluster_d` is the hardest test. Baseline handed it to untrained `vs_d6`
  climbing 0.48→0.84, with trained `vs_d5` never above 0.12 — a strong
  early-feedback lottery win by the wrong spoke. cfgA rep2 got `vs_d5` to 0.96
  at a different seed; flipping cluster_d here would be the strongest single
  result available from this run.
- `cluster_a`: baseline locked `vs_a2` from session 1 onward. rep1's champion
  was trained `vs_a1` at 0.92–1.00, rep2's `vs_a1` was flat 0.00 — this is the
  slot carrying the known replicate variance, so we hold no expectation and
  read it as information about variance rather than about the config.

**If cfgA is baseline-plus-noise**, the signature is 0–1 aligned slots with the
same universal dynamics intact and untrained spokes taking the strong
concentration curves — specifically, cluster winners at session 5 that are
whichever spoke won session 1, regardless of training. Watch `vs_d3`, which has
now produced rising learned-looking curves on an *untrained* spoke in two
separate runs; another `vs_d3` climb here is evidence for concentration without
alignment, i.e. for item C's lock-in hypothesis over a config effect.

We are explicitly **not** treating the universal dynamics as outcomes: seed-share
collapse (~45–55% → 4–17%), within-run judge up-rate decay, a trickle of
cross-cluster/mixed edges, and flat 0.0000/4.0 mean-mode headline metrics have
appeared in all seven learned runs to date and will appear here. Their absence
would be the surprise worth investigating; their presence says nothing about
cfgA.

## How we'll measure

**Primary — §6.1 Learning Measures → Per-Cluster Winner Table (M39) →
Final-Session Cluster Winners**, counting cluster-slots whose final-session
sample-mode argmax winner is the trained spoke. Pre-registered rule, fixed by
`EXPERIMENT-PLAN.md` item B.c before this run:

| rep3 outcome | Pooled cfgA | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| **≥2/4 aligned** | 6/12 | cfgA is a **confirmed directional signal**; becomes the base config for item F's knob experiments. |
| **1/4 aligned** | 5/12 | **Ambiguous** — consult item C's lock-in analysis before choosing the next round's configs. |
| **0/4 aligned** | 4/12 | cfgA is **baseline-plus-noise**. |

Baseline for comparison: 2/12 across the three Round 1 replicates.

**Secondary — champion strength, read under all three outcomes.** Does rep3
produce at least one champion at or above the Round 1 champion band
(`pass_rate ≥ 0.44`)? cfgA's value may lie less in average alignment than in
enabling strong champions, so a 1/4 run with a 0.90+ trained champion is a
different animal from a 1/4 run of weak winners. Same table, all sessions.

**Supporting sections**, read but not decision-bearing on their own:

- **Trained-Subset Comparison (M39/M41), final-session mean Δ vs.
  static_weight** — does rep3 escape the Round 1 envelope of [−0.46, −0.12]?
  Only cfgA rep1 has, at 0.00. Report the pinned-excluded figure alongside it;
  `vs_d5` was control-pinned at this seed under both controls in Round 1, so
  the all-probes number is partly an artifact of pinning.
- **Per-cluster winner table, session 1 vs. session 5** — feeds item C, and
  the paired flip/preserve read against `exp0b-2026-08-06`.
- **§6.3 Data-Integrity / Confound Checks** — seed-fixture provenance,
  graph-wipe verification, probe-replay-index normalization must all pass, and
  `expected_config` must have asserted 3.0/3.0 against the live backend. Any
  failure here voids the run rather than producing a result.
- **Edge Weight Delta Ledger → Edge Classification** — the conv→seed and
  mixed-cluster rows, logged passively for the standing cross-cluster watch
  list and as raw material for item D's anchor-strength metric.

**Caveat pre-registered against the paired read.** Because the baseline pair at
this seed was the weakest of the three (0/4, −0.46 floor), a good rep3 will be
tempting to read as a large paired effect. The primary rule above is immune to
this — alignment is counted absolutely against trained spokes, not as a
difference from the paired baseline — but the *paired narrative* is not. If
rep3 aligns well here, the honest statement is "cfgA aligned N/4 at a sequence
where baseline aligned 0/4," not "cfgA improved alignment by N slots," and any
claim about effect size stays with the pooled 12-slot count. Conversely, a poor
rep3 at this seed should not be excused as "the hard seed" — the rule was fixed
before launch and 0/4 means 4/12.

## Not settled by this run

Whether the effect, if confirmed, is a *config* effect or a shift in
early-feedback lottery odds. Item C's lock-in analysis is the instrument for
that question and should land before this run's read; if the two disagree —
rep3 aligns well *and* lock-in comes back strong — the read needs both on the
table, since "config shifts session-1 odds" and "config sustains a
session-1 win" are different mechanisms with different implications for
item F's knob search.
